## Follow-up 1 (2026-10-29): two verify workers, and failure names in the summary

**Measured** by the first `sigma-cloud` diagnostic session (Sigma 1.0.24, checkout at `6df8c2b`, 4 vCPUs,
15.7 GiB total and 15.2 GiB available; the session reported 16.9 and 16.3 GB): one `loop.py verify` of the
command above ran on **one** xdist worker (`gw0` only) for 2h16m25s (8,185 s). It exited 1: 18 failed, 23,831
passed, 89 skipped, 9 xfailed. The lease peaked at 3.9 GiB. Trust came from the environment. The 18 failures are
out of scope here; they get their own issue once named.

**Why one worker (this corrects the design above).** This suite answers `-n auto` itself.
`tests/conftest.py`'s `pytest_xdist_auto_num_workers` returns `max(1, min(cpus, MemAvailable_MiB // 4 //
SIGMA_TEST_WORKER_MB))`, at 2000 MB per worker by default, and about 15,500 MiB gives 1. xdist's own implementation
reads `PYTEST_XDIST_AUTO_NUM_WORKERS`, but it runs last, so the resource guard's grant is never read for this
suite. That was measured offline with pytest 9.2.0 and xdist 3.8.0: a conftest hook returning 2, the variable set
to 1, and `-n auto` gave `created: 2/2 workers`. The cold-ring lease of 1 was the same number by coincidence. The
design above, `setup.sh` and the README said "the worker count the resource guard grants". That was wrong, and
this change corrects it.

**Change.** In `VERIFY`, `-rs -q -n auto` becomes `-rsEf -q -n 2`.
- `-n 2` is a literal, which neither the conftest hook nor the guard's cold ring lowers. Doctor's `resource
  verify workers` row is red under `-n auto` and green under `-n 2` here (3 derived).
- `-rsEf` names failures in the summary. pytest's `-r` replaces its default `fE`, so `-rs` printed skips only.
  The summary prints in the order of the letters. Sigma keeps the last five lines of output as the evidence
  `tail` (`loop.py`), so failures come last, directly above the count line. With 89 skips, `-rfEs` would have
  filled that tail with SKIPPED lines.

**Why two and not three.** On the next verify in the same container, the guard admits a literal only if that many
workers fit. It learns a run's cost as `peak / max(workers, sizeable processes)`. The sizeable processes are
counted at release, when the verify's group has already exited, or by another admission during the run, which a
one-session container does not have. So the unit is the worker count, and the room has to hold the first run's
whole peak. The room is `min(available - 0.2 x total, 0.6 x total)`, about 9.4 GiB here, since one session leases
nothing else. One worker's whole tree peaked at 3.9 GiB. Three workers come to about 11.7 GiB by tripling it.
That is an estimate, not a bound, since every worker collects the whole suite. It is above the room, so a fix-up
verify in that container could hold, and then, after about two holds, run three workers anyway on a cost that said
they do not fit (see "What a hold does mid-goal"). Two come to about 7.8 GiB by the same estimate, which fits. Three is
the next step once a two-worker peak is measured. At best the verify takes about 70 minutes on two workers (half
of 2h16m, assuming perfect scaling).

**Overriding the conftest's memory rule.** The hook budgets a quarter of available memory at 2000 MB per worker.
It is sized for a shared laptop: a fixed `-n 4` once ran a 24 GB Mac out of memory. Under that rule, two workers
need 16,000 MiB available, and the container has about 15,500. The container runs one session and nothing else.

**Backstop: the guard's shed, with three caveats.** The template leaves `resource.mode` and `resource.shed` null,
so the mode is `enforce` and the shed is on. If the machine stays critical, meaning available memory is below half the reserve (about 1.6 GiB), for three
samples, the guard stops the verify's own process group and the run is a hold, not an out-of-memory kill. The
caveats:
- The three samples take about 6 s, so a fast allocation can meet the kernel's OOM killer first.
- A shed run's peak is still recorded, so the next attempt or two hold (see below).
- The shed lasts only while nobody sets `SIGMA_RESOURCE_MODE` to `observe` or `off`.

**Options rejected.**
- `-n 3`: see above.
- `SIGMA_TEST_WORKER_MB`, which only the conftest reads: it misstates the per-worker cost, and as an environment
  variable it is one more operator step.
- `-n {workers}`, the form doctor recommends: the guard grants one unit on a cold ring, and a cloud container's
  ring is empty at its first verify.
- `resource.max_verify_workers`: it caps the count and never raises it.
- Seeding the cost ring from setup: it leaves root-owned state behind and puts an unmeasured cost into the ring.

**What a hold does mid-goal.** `loop.py verify` exits 5 with no evidence. `land.md` handles a `HOLD` only at the
pick, so what a `--land` run does after a verify holds is not specified; `docs/configuration.md` says to retry
after the stated wait. A hold does not last. The literal-count hold returns inside `resource_verify.run`'s `try`,
whose `finally` releases the lease, and `_finish` records the lease holder's own footprint, a few MiB, into both
rings. After two holds, `learned_cost` drops the real measurement as an outlier (above 4x the median), and the next
attempt runs the literal count. That was measured by the post-PR review on a fake 15.7 GiB machine: hold, hold,
ran with 2 workers, learned cost 11 MiB. From then on the shed is the only protection. The README says this; the
product fix is a follow-up.

**Follow-up to file (enqueue-ready, separate from this PR).** Two gaps:
- Sigma's own suite never honours the guard's grant under `-n auto`, because the conftest hook answers first.

`PYTEST_XDIST_AUTO_NUM_WORKERS` when it is set, would fix both.
