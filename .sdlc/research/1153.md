## Cause 1 and 2 (test_overrun_stops_the_whole_group, test_term_comes_before_kill)
`run_group` computes `deadline = clock() + timeout` AFTER `Popen`, the lifeline write and selector setup. The tests inject `clock = 1e6 if pidfile.exists() else 0.0`. If the command announces (writes its pidfile) before `run_group` reaches its first `clock()` call, the deadline is computed as 1e6+60, every later clock() reads 1e6, `remaining` stays 59.99 s positive, and the run waits the full 60 s and reports `ok`. Under load the child wins the race. Evidence: failure shows `seconds=60.0037`, outcome `ok`; the direct-kill control's grandchild "is gone" because its `sleep 60` simply finished. Instrumented clock on an unloaded run shows calls 1,2 = 0.0 then 1e6 (the intended order).
Product relevance: the budget is measured from after the spawn, not from before it, so a slow spawn path is not counted. Taking the deadline before `Popen` is correct and removes the race.

## Cause 3 (test_split_mode_peak_memory_under_a_flood_is_bounded) - a wrong measurement, shown
`ru_maxrss` of an exec'd child on Linux inherits the high-water RSS of the parent's mm at exec (vfork shares the parent mm). A pytest xdist worker after thousands of tests has ~900 MB RSS. Measured in Docker: parent holding 900 MiB, child prints `ru_maxrss=932568` KiB but `/proc/self/status VmHWM=8312` kB. CI saw 951248/1015596 KiB. `VmHWM` is per-mm and starts fresh at exec, so it is the right Linux reading; macOS keeps `ru_maxrss` (bytes).
